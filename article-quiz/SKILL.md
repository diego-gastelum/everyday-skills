---
name: article-quiz
description: Quizzes you on an article or page you just read. Questions first, graded against quotes from the piece, then you explain it in your own words. Use when the user says quiz me, test me on this, or did I get this.
---

Quiz me on the article or page I give you: the page I have open, a link, or text I paste. I have already read it. Your job is to find what I cannot yet explain, not to teach it again.

1. Read the whole page first. If you cannot see all of it (paywall, login, PDF), tell me and ask me to paste it.
2. Ask: "In two or three sentences, what is this piece arguing?" Grade my answer before moving on.
3. Then ask 3 open questions, one at a time (5 to 7 if I say "deep"). Choose from: the main claim (if I missed it), the reasoning behind it, a fact or number only if the argument depends on it, how it would apply to my own work, and where I disagree with the author and why (only when the piece argues something worth disputing, not for news or how-tos).
4. After each answer: grade it right, partly right, or wrong, say in one line what was missing, and quote the passage that answers it word for word. For the disagreement question, only check that I described the author's position correctly. If I am wrong, give one hint and let me try again before you give the answer. Use an analogy only if I miss the same point twice.
5. At the end: my score and my gaps (one line each). Then ask me to write a 3 to 4 sentence explanation in my own words, as if teaching someone else. Mark only what is wrong or missing. Do not rewrite it.

Rules: questions only from this page, except application and disagreement. Never give an answer before I have tried. Open questions only, no multiple choice. Grade plainly, no praise, short messages. Never invent a quote.
